## 🌟 Project Vision

Every portrait carries a story. A grandmother's laugh lines. A child's first school photograph. A professional headshot meant to open doors. Anthropic Portrait Studio 2026 was conceived around a single guiding question: *what if the software respected the story as much as the subject?*

Instead of overwhelming users with a hundred sliders, this project favors a curated set of intelligent adjustments that adapt to the photograph in front of them. The interface breathes. The workflow flows. The results feel less like manipulation and more like collaboration between the photographer's intent and the software's understanding.

We believe portrait software should feel like a well-lit studio rather than a crowded switchboard.

---

## 🧩 What Makes This Different

Most portrait utilities on the market assume you already know exactly what you want. They hand you a toolbox and walk away. Anthropic Portrait Studio 2026 takes the opposite approach — it studies the image, recognizes context, and gently suggests a direction. You remain in control, but you're never alone at the controls.

- **Guided Creativity** — suggestions appear contextually, not as pop-ups demanding attention.
- **Non-Destructive Workflow** — every adjustment is layered and reversible at any point.
- **Intent Awareness** — the tool distinguishes between a casual selfie and a formal portrait, adjusting its defaults accordingly.
- **Natural Language Descriptions** — settings are explained in plain prose rather than cryptic technical labels.

## 🎯 Responsive Interface Design

The interface is not merely "resizable." It is genuinely responsive in the way modern web design has taught us to expect. Panels reposition themselves, controls consolidate at smaller widths, and the layout prioritizes whichever tools you use most frequently. On a 13-inch laptop, the interface feels focused. On a 32-inch display, it feels expansive without becoming empty.

Design principles guiding the layout:

1. **Progressive Disclosure** — advanced options appear only when the current task calls for them.
2. **Visual Hierarchy** — the most-used controls occupy the most reachable areas.
3. **Consistent Spacing** — a strict rhythm keeps the eye comfortable.
4. **Calm Color** — muted neutrals let your photograph remain the focus.

## 🧠 How It Works Under the Hood

Without diving into proprietary implementation details, it is worth explaining the conceptual architecture that powers the experience.

The application is organized into three cooperating layers:

1. **The Perception Layer** — interprets the image, identifying subject, background, lighting direction, and tonal distribution.
2. **The Suggestion Layer** — translates perception into a small set of context-appropriate recommendations.
3. **The Rendering Layer** — applies changes non-destructively, preserving the original data at all times.

This layered design means that changing a suggestion never forces a full re-render, and manual refinements always take precedence over automated ones. The architecture was chosen deliberately to keep the artist in charge while minimizing the friction of routine tasks.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting Guide

Occasionally, things do not behave as expected. Below are common situations and their explanations.

- **Interface appears blurry on a high-DPI monitor** — verify that the display scaling matches the system recommendation.
- **Performance feels sluggish on large files** — enable hardware acceleration in the preferences panel.
- **Language does not switch immediately** — a restart is required for right-to-left scripts to mirror fully.
- **Stylus input feels delayed** — check for conflicting tablet drivers from other vendors.
- **Exported colors look different from the editor** — confirm the color profile matches the destination medium.

If a problem persists, the support channels listed above are always available.
